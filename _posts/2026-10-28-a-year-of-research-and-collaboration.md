Over the past year, Ateneo de Manila University has continued its involvement in TEMPEST through research activities, international academic exchanges, and collaborations examining the intersections of economic development, environmental challenges, and institutional resilience in the Philippines.

The collaboration began with the [“Firms and Economic Development” workshop]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url 2025-08-04-kick-off-workshop-on-firms-and-economic-development %}), held on August 4, 2025, at Ateneo’s Loyola Heights campus in Manila. Hosted at Faber Hall, the workshop brought together researchers from academic institutions and policy organizations to discuss issues in economic development. It featured keynote presentations by Professor Toshihiro Okubo of Keio University and Professor Heiwai Tang of the University of Hong Kong, alongside contributions from scholars affiliated with the World Bank, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, Seoul National University, the University of the Philippines, and Ateneo de Manila University.

Following the workshop, the Ateneo research team undertook months of fieldwork in Marikina City to examine the evolution of local disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) institutions. Through key informant interviews with local government officials and community-level stakeholders, the research explored how disaster preparedness, emergency response, and institutional coordination have developed over time. Particular attention was given to the ways national policy frameworks have been adapted to local circumstances, as well as the role of institutional experience and community participation in strengthening disaster governance.

The project’s international collaboration continued in May and June 2026, when Dr. Maire Carroline Magante of Ateneo de Manila University and Dr. Anthony Sabarillo of the University of the Philippines spent a month as [guest researchers at Oslo Metropolitan University]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url 2026-06-30-research-visit-to-oslomet %}). Their visit included research meetings, writing sessions, conferences, and academic exchanges with colleagues. Together with TEMPEST Project Manager Fenella Carpena, they also visited the Philippine Embassy in Oslo to present the TEMPEST research with Ambassador Enrico Fos and Consul Glenn Obach. The visit further provided opportunities to participate in academic conferences in Stockholm and Växjö, Sweden.

In September 2026, Dr. Magante was [awarded the Ricardo Leong Grant]({{ site.baseurl }}{% post_url 2026-09-28-dr-maire-magante-awarded-ricardo-leong-research-grant %}) for Global Area Studies Research for the academic year 2026–2027. Her study, “Climate Shocks and Internal Migration: Evidence from Typhoon Exposure in the Philippines,” examines the relationship between typhoon exposure and internal migration. Undertaken in partnership with TEMPEST and OsloMet, the research represents a further opportunity to strengthen international collaboration on the economic consequences of climate-related shocks.

Looking ahead, the Ateneo team composed of Dr. Magante and Mr. Rainier Gerald Mora is scheduled to present findings from its TEMPEST research at the East Asian Economic Association (EAEA) Conference 2026, to be held in Siem Reap, Cambodia, in November. The presentation will provide an opportunity to share insights developed through the project’s research activities and engage with a wider community of scholars working on economic development and related policy challenges.

Together, these activities reflect an ongoing collaboration between Ateneo and its TEMPEST partners, bringing together field-based research, academic exchange, and policy-relevant inquiry to deepen understanding of development and environmental challenges in the Philippines.
